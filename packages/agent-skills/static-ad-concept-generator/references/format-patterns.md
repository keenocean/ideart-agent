# Platform Format Patterns

Use this guide for durable creative patterns and cross-platform adaptation. Platform specifications are volatile: supported placements, dimensions, aspect ratios, duration, card count, text limits, and safe zones can change independently of this skill package.

## Freshness rule

Before presenting an exact platform requirement or producing a supposedly compliant asset:

1. Use `web_search` to locate current official platform documentation on the platform's official domain.
2. Use `web_fetch` on that official page rather than relying on a third-party summary.
3. Record the platform, placement, specification source, and verification date in production notes.
4. If current verification is unavailable, use user-supplied requirements or clearly labeled working assumptions.
5. Recheck every derived output rather than assuming one master canvas satisfies all placements.

This file deliberately avoids a static inventory of platform limits. Treat it as a strategy library, not a compliance source.

## Platform creative patterns

### Meta surfaces

- single product hero with a short headline;
- problem, solution, and proof sequence across cards;
- evidence-backed review or demonstration layout;
- product comparison only when the compared facts are supported;
- vertical story treatment with UI-safe copy placement.

### Google and programmatic display

- reduce the idea to product, one message, and one CTA;
- prioritize contrast and legibility at small rendered sizes;
- keep brand recognition without allowing the logo to dominate the message;
- adapt the composition to each inventory shape instead of stretching one image.

### TikTok surfaces

- native-feeling first frame and immediate visual or text hook;
- demonstration, reaction, list, or problem-to-product structure;
- concise overlays that preserve comprehension without sound;
- product behavior and claims grounded in available evidence.

### LinkedIn surfaces

- insight-led or evidence-led headline;
- case-study snapshot only when metrics are verified;
- professional visual hierarchy without dense presentation-slide copy;
- educational sequence when the chosen placement supports multiple pages or cards.

### Pinterest surfaces

- product in a useful or aspirational context;
- tutorial, checklist, or step-by-step information hierarchy;
- clear relationship between the image, headline, and destination intent;
- enough vertical breathing room for discovery-oriented browsing.

### YouTube companion surfaces

- visual continuity with the related video campaign;
- one recognizable product or brand cue;
- minimal copy that remains legible beside or over video;
- placement-specific crop and overlay review.

## Reusable layout patterns

### Feed or card

Use one dominant image, one headline, one support element, and one CTA. The image should communicate the subject before the viewer reads the copy.

### Full-screen vertical

Keep the hook and product inside the central content area. Reserve breathing room around the edges for platform UI, captions, and controls after verifying the current safe zones.

### Compact banner

Remove secondary claims and decorative details. Preserve only the product, the core message, and the action. Recompose for each shape.

### Carousel or sequence

Give each card one job. A common progression is tension → mechanism → proof → product → action, but use only the beats the evidence supports.

### Comparison

Keep compared attributes symmetrical and sourced. Avoid manipulated scales, fake competitors, or unsupported superiority language.

### Proof-led creative

Use a real demonstration, approved testimonial, verified statistic, or product detail. Mark legal qualifiers and their minimum readable treatment in the production brief.

## Cross-platform adaptation workflow

1. Preserve the master concept: audience tension, angle, evidence, and CTA.
2. Choose the exact platform and placement.
3. Verify current official specifications.
4. Recompose the hierarchy for that placement and viewing context.
5. Recheck crop, text size, product visibility, legal copy, and UI-safe areas.
6. Change one major test variable at a time so results remain interpretable.

Do not infer that a pattern, placement, or specification is current merely because it appears in an older campaign or reference ad.
