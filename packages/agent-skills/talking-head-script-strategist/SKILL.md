# Talking Head Script Strategist

Write scripts that sound spoken rather than read. This is a planning skill and does not render an avatar, clone a voice, or generate media.

## Inputs

Use the product, audience, platform, language, duration, tone, proof, offer, CTA, presenter constraints, and approved pronunciation. If claims or consent are missing, mark the gap. Never invent testimonials, results, credentials, scarcity, or a real-person endorsement.

## Pacing

Use these ranges as guides, then read the script aloud mentally:

| Delivery       | Words/second | 30-second range |
| -------------- | -----------: | --------------: |
| Educational    |      1.8–2.2 |           54–66 |
| Professional   |      2.0–2.5 |           60–75 |
| Conversational |      2.5–3.0 |           75–90 |
| Energetic      |      3.0–3.5 |          90–105 |

Choose clarity over filling every second. Leave space for product shots, reaction beats, captions, and a clean end card.

## Structures

### 15 seconds

Hook → bridge → one benefit or demonstration → CTA.

### 30 seconds

Hook → recognizable problem → product/mechanism → proof or demonstration → CTA.

### 60 seconds

Hook → short story/problem → discovery → two or three grounded benefits → proof/objection answer → CTA.

## Voice and authenticity

- Use short sentences, contractions, direct address, and occasional fragments.
- Make transitions conversational without adding filler to every line.
- Give the presenter a credible relationship to the audience and product.
- Match vocabulary to the market without stereotyping age, region, or identity.
- Write difficult product names phonetically in production notes, not in on-screen copy.
- Mark pauses, emphasis, emotion, and intended accent only when the user has supplied or approved them.
- Do not imitate a named living person or imply an endorsement.

## Scene plan

Choose the smallest useful structure:

- Two scenes: hook/presenter, then product/proof/CTA.
- Three scenes: hook, demonstration or proof, CTA.
- Five scenes: hook, problem, product reveal, proof, CTA.

For every scene give timing, spoken line, visual action, framing, product visibility, on-screen text, and transition. Spoken and on-screen copy should reinforce rather than duplicate each other word for word.

## Output

Return:

1. creative premise and persona rationale;
2. timed final script with estimated word count and duration;
3. scene map;
4. delivery, pronunciation, caption, and B-roll notes;
5. claim or approval gaps;
6. two alternative hooks and one alternative CTA.

Keep the core promise stable across variants so later tests isolate the hook or CTA instead of changing the whole strategy.
